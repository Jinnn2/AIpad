Task A: Bio-Architecture (Structural Adaptation)
Phase 1: Reading Material (8 Minutes)
Title: Trabecular Bone Principles and Topology Optimization
Core Concept: Wolff’s Law
Human bones are not static; they follow "Wolff's Law," which states that bone grows and remodels in response to the loads placed upon it. Specifically, the Trabecular bone (the spongy inner layer) forms a lattice-like structure. The struts of this lattice, called trabeculae, align themselves precisely along the lines of maximum stress. This creates a structure that is "anisotropic"—meaning it is incredibly strong in the direction of common pressure but lightweight overall.
Technical Application: Topology Optimization (TO)
In modern engineering, we use "Topology Optimization" to mimic this biological efficiency. TO is a mathematical method that optimizes material layout within a given physical design space for a set of loads. Instead of making a solid beam, the algorithm "carves away" unnecessary material, leaving a bone-like web.

Topic B: Digital Economics (Network Effects & Lock-in)
Phase 1: Reading Material (8 Minutes)
Title: The Dynamics of Platform Ecosystems
Core Concept: Network Effects (Metcalfe’s Law)
A network effect occurs when a product or service becomes more valuable to its users as more people use it. Direct Network Effects occur when an increase in usage leads to a direct increase in value for other users (e.g., a social network). Indirect Network Effects occur when more users attract more "complementary" goods or third-party developers (e.g., more gamers attracting more game developers to a console), which in turn increases the value for the users.
The Lock-in Mechanism
As a platform grows, it creates Switching Costs. These aren't just financial; they include Data Lock-in (all your photos and history are there) and Search Costs (learning a new interface). High switching costs lead to "Lock-in," making it difficult for a superior technical competitor to unseat an incumbent.
Term	Definition	Impact on Competition
Critical Mass	The point where the system becomes self-sustaining.	Hard for new startups to reach.
Multi-homing	Users using multiple competing platforms at once.	Weakens the incumbent's lock-in.

Topic C: Environmental Engineering (Urban Heat Island - UHI)
Phase 1: Reading Material (8 Minutes)
Title: Mitigating the Urban Heat Island (UHI) Effect
The Physics of UHI
Urban areas are significantly warmer than surrounding rural areas. This is primarily due to Albedo (the reflectivity of surfaces) and Thermal Mass. Dark surfaces like asphalt have low albedo, absorbing up to 90% of solar radiation. Meanwhile, materials like concrete act as a "Heat Battery," storing energy during the day and releasing it at night.
Evapotranspiration & Vegetation
Rural areas stay cool because of plants. Plants perform Evapotranspiration, a process where they release water vapor into the air, which consumes heat energy and cools the surrounding environment. Urban "Grey Infrastructure" lacks this cooling mechanism, leading to higher energy consumption for air conditioning.
Mitigation Strategy	Mechanism	Primary Challenge
Cool Roofs	Increase Albedo (reflective paint)	Glare to neighboring buildings.
Permeable Pavement	Water evaporation from soil beneath	Lower structural load capacity.
Green Infrastructure	Shading + Evapotranspiration	High maintenance/water demand.
